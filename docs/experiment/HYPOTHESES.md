# 108-Day Experiment Hypotheses

**Status:** Active experiment specification\
**Experiment period:** 7 September 2026 -- 23 December 2026\
**Constitution reference:** `docs/experiment/EXPERIMENT.md`\
**Hypothesis version:** v1.0

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the hypotheses tested by the 108-Day Experiment.

The hypotheses are deliberately separated from the experiment
constitution so that:

-   the questions being tested remain explicit;
-   observations can be distinguished from explanations;
-   supporting and challenging evidence can both be recorded;
-   alternative explanations can be considered;
-   the final conclusion is not predetermined.

The experiment is designed to **test** these hypotheses, not to assume
that they are true.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# H1 --- Sustained Protocol Execution and Financial Outcomes

### Hypothesis

> **Sustained execution of the 108-day protocol will materially improve
> financial outcomes.**

### Primary question

Does consistent execution of the defined 108-day protocol coincide with
a material improvement in financial outcomes?

### Target outcome

The principal financial target is:

> **₹3,00,000 per month**

This is an outcome target and must not be treated as proof of the
hypothesis.

### Relevant observations

The experiment should record:

-   protocol adherence;
-   successful days;
-   focused work;
-   deep work;
-   sales attempts;
-   proposals;
-   leads;
-   customers;
-   content/output;
-   revenue;
-   cash received;
-   expenses;
-   profit;
-   opportunities;
-   opportunity value;
-   other relevant observable work and financial activity.

### Evidence supporting H1

Examples include:

-   financial outcomes materially improve during sustained protocol
    execution;
-   improvement is accompanied by sustained execution;
-   improvements remain visible across multiple periods rather than
    appearing as a single isolated event;
-   observable behavioral changes plausibly connect protocol execution
    with financial activity.

### Evidence challenging H1

Examples include:

-   financial outcomes do not materially improve despite high protocol
    adherence;
-   financial outcomes decline despite sustained execution;
-   financial improvements occur only when unrelated external factors
    change;
-   financial outcomes show no meaningful relationship with protocol
    execution.

### Alternative explanations to consider

Potential alternatives include:

-   increased work volume;
-   changes in pricing;
-   new products or services;
-   marketing activity;
-   networking;
-   existing business pipeline;
-   market conditions;
-   seasonal effects;
-   one-off transactions;
-   previously initiated opportunities;
-   changes in available time or resources.

### Assessment

H1 may be classified at the end as:

-   **Supported**
-   **Partially supported**
-   **Inconclusive**
-   **Not supported**

# H2 --- Internal State, Behavior and Execution

### Hypothesis

> **Repeatedly entering and internalizing the psychological state
> associated with already achieving the goal will improve focus,
> behavior, opportunity recognition and execution.**

### Primary question

Does repeated 3-6-9 goal practice and associated state work coincide
with measurable improvements in internal state, behavior, opportunity
recognition, and execution?

### Intervention

The daily 3-6-9 practice uses the goal statement:

> **I create 3 lakh per month through focused, valuable work that I
> consistently execute.**

The planned schedule is:

-   Morning: 3 repetitions
-   Afternoon: 6 repetitions
-   Evening: 9 repetitions

Total:

**18 repetitions per day**

Planned total over 108 days:

**1,944 repetitions**

### Relevant observations

Record, where practical:

-   completion of repetitions;
-   state before practice;
-   state after practice;
-   focus;
-   confidence;
-   clarity;
-   calm;
-   certainty;
-   energy;
-   visualization;
-   feelings;
-   insights;
-   subsequent work behavior;
-   sales behavior;
-   opportunity recognition;
-   actions taken.

### Evidence supporting H2

Examples include:

-   measurable improvement in focus during the experiment;
-   increased consistency of valuable work;
-   increased observable execution;
-   increased recognition or recording of opportunities;
-   changes in behavior that consistently follow the practice;
-   improvements in relevant internal-state measures.

### Evidence challenging H2

Examples include:

-   no measurable change in focus or execution;
-   internal-state scores improve without corresponding behavioral
    improvement;
-   behavioral improvements occur independently of 3-6-9 practice;
-   repeated practice produces no observable change;
-   improvements disappear when practice adherence decreases.

### Important limitation

Self-reported internal state is subjective.

A change in a self-reported feeling does not by itself establish that an
external behavioral or financial effect was caused by the practice.

# H3 --- Health, Discipline and Cross-Domain Effects

### Hypothesis

> **The health/discipline transformation will produce improvements in
> other areas of life through interconnected behavioral mechanisms.**

### Primary question

Does sustained execution of the protocol produce measurable improvements
in health and discipline that are accompanied by improvements in work,
mental state, or other observable domains?

### Relevant health and discipline measures

Where available:

-   weight;
-   waist;
-   running distance;
-   running duration;
-   running performance;
-   exercise execution;
-   sleep duration;
-   sleep quality;
-   fasting adherence;
-   nutrition adherence;
-   energy;
-   protocol adherence.

### Relevant cross-domain measures

-   focused work;
-   deep work;
-   task completion;
-   sales activity;
-   opportunity recognition;
-   confidence;
-   clarity;
-   financial outcomes.

### Evidence supporting H3

Examples include:

-   health and physical execution improve;
-   improved physical execution is accompanied by increased work
    consistency;
-   improvements in energy or sleep coincide with improved focus;
-   increased discipline appears across multiple domains;
-   health/discipline changes precede or accompany observable behavioral
    changes.

### Evidence challenging H3

Examples include:

-   health improves without meaningful improvement elsewhere;
-   protocol execution improves but work behavior remains unchanged;
-   improvements in other domains appear unrelated to health or
    discipline changes;
-   no meaningful cross-domain relationship is observed.

### Alternative explanations

Cross-domain changes may result from:

-   changes in workload;
-   environmental changes;
-   external incentives;
-   business opportunities;
-   changes in schedule;
-   unrelated lifestyle changes;
-   natural variation.

The experiment should not automatically interpret simultaneous
improvement as evidence of causation.

# H4 --- Financial Improvement Beyond Observable Behavioral Mechanisms

### Status

**Exploratory**

### Hypothesis

> **Financial improvement will occur beyond what can be reasonably
> explained by observable behavioral changes.**

### Why H4 is difficult

This is the most difficult hypothesis to evaluate because financial
outcomes can arise from many interacting causes.

The experiment can observe:

-   work;
-   sales activity;
-   proposals;
-   leads;
-   customers;
-   networking;
-   opportunities;
-   execution;
-   protocol adherence;
-   internal-state reports.

It cannot directly observe every causal factor affecting financial
outcomes.

Therefore, H4 must remain exploratory.

### Primary question

After accounting for observable behavioral and business activity, is
there a meaningful financial improvement that remains unexplained by
those observable mechanisms?

### Evidence that could support H4

Evidence would need to show, as far as the available data permits:

1.  A meaningful financial improvement occurred.
2.  Observable behavioral changes do not adequately account for the
    magnitude or timing of the improvement.
3.  The improvement is not readily explained by known external factors.
4.  The observation is not merely a single unusual event.
5.  Alternative explanations have been actively considered.

### Evidence challenging H4

H4 should be considered unsupported when financial improvement can
reasonably be explained by observable factors such as:

-   increased focused work;
-   more sales attempts;
-   more proposals;
-   more leads;
-   new customers;
-   new products;
-   improved conversion;
-   pricing changes;
-   networking;
-   existing pipeline;
-   market conditions;
-   seasonal effects;
-   one-off transactions;
-   other identifiable business mechanisms.

### Critical rule

**Unexplained does not mean supernatural.**

If the experiment cannot identify a cause, the correct classification
is:

> **Cause not identified**

not:

> **Manifestation caused the outcome**

### Strongest possible conclusion

Even if H4 appears consistent with the data, the experiment should
describe the result as:

> **Financial improvement not fully explained by the measured behavioral
> variables and known alternative explanations.**

It should not claim proof of a metaphysical mechanism.

# 5. Hypothesis Relationship Model

The hypotheses are related but should not be collapsed into one
hypothesis.

The intended conceptual chain is:

``` text
108-Day Protocol
       ↓
Internal State
       ↓
Behavior / Execution
       ↓
Opportunities
       ↓
Financial Outcomes
```

Health and discipline may influence multiple points in this chain:

``` text
Health / Discipline
        ↓
 ┌──────┼────────┐
 ↓      ↓        ↓
Mind   Work   Execution
 └──────┼────────┘
        ↓
   Opportunities
        ↓
    Financial
    Outcomes
```

This is a conceptual model, not an established causal pathway.

# 6. What Would Count as a Stronger Result?

A stronger result would include:

-   high protocol adherence;
-   reliable daily records;
-   measurable improvement in relevant behaviors;
-   measurable improvement in financial outcomes;
-   repeated patterns over time;
-   clear temporal ordering where available;
-   few major missing-data periods;
-   plausible mechanisms connecting protocol to behavior;
-   consideration of competing explanations.

A weaker result would include:

-   substantial missing data;
-   inconsistent protocol execution;
-   major changes to the protocol;
-   major external changes;
-   isolated financial events;
-   subjective interpretations without observable behavioral evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Confounding and Alternative Explanations

The following must be considered before making strong conclusions:

### Business activity

A financial increase may result from ordinary business activity rather
than the protocol.

### Existing pipeline

Revenue may have originated from work initiated before the experiment.

### Market conditions

Market demand or external economic conditions may change independently
of the experiment.

### Seasonality

Certain months may naturally produce different business outcomes.

### New strategies

Introducing a new business model, product, service, marketing channel,
or pricing strategy may independently affect revenue.

### Measurement changes

Improved tracking may create the appearance of change because
measurement became more accurate.

### Regression to the mean

An unusually weak or strong baseline can naturally move toward a more
typical level.

### Selection and attention effects

The act of recording opportunities may itself increase awareness of
opportunities.

These explanations do not invalidate the experiment. They determine how
strongly its results can be interpreted.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Evidence Classification

Each significant observation should be classified where practical as:

-   **Supporting**
-   **Challenging**
-   **Neutral**
-   **Alternative explanation**
-   **Unknown**

This prevents every interesting event from being automatically counted
as evidence for the hypothesis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Confidence

Confidence should be recorded separately from the result.

A simple scale may be used:

  Confidence   Meaning
  ------------ ----------------------
  1--2/10      Very weak evidence
  3--4/10      Weak evidence
  5--6/10      Moderate / uncertain
  7--8/10      Strong evidence
  9--10/10     Very strong evidence

Confidence is an assessment of the evidence, not a measurement of
belief.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Final Assessment Rules

At Day 108, each hypothesis must be evaluated independently.

The final report should contain:

``` text
Hypothesis
Result
Evidence supporting
Evidence challenging
Alternative explanations
Missing data
Limitations
Confidence
```

The system must be capable of returning:

> **Not supported**

for any hypothesis.

It must also be capable of returning:

> **Inconclusive**

when the available evidence is insufficient.

Neither outcome is considered a failure of the experiment.

A hypothesis that survives a genuine attempt to disprove it has greater
evidentiary value than one that was protected from disconfirming
evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Prohibited Conclusions

The experiment must not automatically conclude:

-   "Manifestation worked."
-   "The 3-6-9 method caused the money."
-   "The protocol caused the revenue increase."
-   "An unexplained event proves a supernatural cause."
-   "Correlation proves causation."
-   "A successful day caused a successful financial day."

Such statements require evidence beyond simple temporal association.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Required Final Questions

At Day 108, the experiment should answer:

1.  Did protocol execution improve?
2.  Did health improve?
3.  Did mental/internal-state measures change?
4.  Did focused and valuable work increase?
5.  Did opportunity recognition or opportunity volume change?
6.  Did financial outcomes improve?
7.  Did the ₹3,00,000/month target get achieved?
8.  What observable behavioral mechanisms accompanied the financial
    change?
9.  What evidence supports each hypothesis?
10. What evidence challenges each hypothesis?
11. What alternative explanations remain?
12. What cannot be explained from the collected data?
13. Which hypotheses are supported, partially supported, inconclusive,
    or not supported?
14. What should be tested next?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Version Control

**Hypothesis version:** v1.0

Changes to the hypotheses after the experiment begins must be explicitly
recorded.

A hypothesis amendment must include:

-   date;
-   previous wording;
-   new wording;
-   reason for change;
-   impact on interpretation;
-   approval.

Historical observations must not be reinterpreted as though the amended
hypothesis had existed from Day 1.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Core Principle

> **The experiment is successful if it produces honest evidence,
> regardless of whether the hypotheses are supported.**

The purpose is not to prove the desired outcome.

The purpose is to discover what happens when the protocol is executed
consistently for 108 days.
